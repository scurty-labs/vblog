An empty blog is hard to judge. You cannot tell whether a heading is too large, whether the line length is comfortable, or whether a theme actually suits you until there is real text on the page. This is sample content: four posts, two pages, a handful of tags and a few public-domain pictures, so you can look around, click things and decide what to keep.

Everything here is ordinary blog content. Edit it, delete it, or leave it in place until you have written something of your own.

## What vblog is

vblog is a small, single-user blogging engine written in V on top of veb and SQLite. There is no account system to configure beyond your own login, no plugin marketplace and no build step for writing: you open the editor, type Markdown and press save. The whole site, including its CSS and fonts, is served from your own server, so a post loads without waiting on anyone else.

It aims to cover the boring parts well:

- Posts and pages with a Markdown editor and a live preview
- Drafts, scheduled publishing and a "publish now" switch
- Tags, a tag cloud in the sidebar and related posts at the end of an article
- A media library with drag-and-drop uploads, titles and alt text
- Ten glass themes that share one layout but not one mood
- An RSS feed and a sitemap without extra configuration

## A tour in five minutes

1. Open `/admin` and log in with the credentials you just chose.
2. Write a post, give it a couple of tags and save it as a draft.
3. Open `/media` and drag in a picture, then copy its link into the post.
4. Publish the draft and see it appear on the front page.
5. Open `/settings` to change the title, description, accent colour and theme.

Nothing is hidden behind a build step, so any change is visible on the next page load.

## The ten themes

| Theme | Mood |
| --- | --- |
| default | warm and neutral, the plainest of the set |
| glass | frosted panels over a soft gradient |
| dark | low light, high contrast |
| sepia | paper and ink |
| ocean | cool blues and teal |
| noir | black, white and a single accent |
| sakura | pale pinks and warm grey |
| forest | greens, moss and bark |
| neon | saturated edges on near-black |
| sunset | amber and violet |

All ten share the same flush-left rail, so switching themes never reshuffles your content — it only changes the palette, the type and how the glass is tinted.
